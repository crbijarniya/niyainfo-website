What we install at every weighbridge

Built to the Mining Department's SOP

Under Rule 73A of the Rajasthan Minor Mineral Concession Rules, 2017, the Department of Mines and Geology has issued a detailed SOP for automating weighbridges. It fixes the hardware, its specifications, where each device goes, the pole heights, the cabling and the site's power and internet needs.

We work only to that SOP. Our teams have automated more than 100 weighbridges across Rajasthan, so the layout, the installation and the eRawanna configuration are done right the first time and the site clears inspection without rework.

What happens when a truck arrives

The whole weighment is recorded without anyone typing a vehicle number or a weight.

  1. Truck is identified

    The RFID reader reads the RFID tag or FASTag on the vehicle's mirror from 3 to 6 metres away.

  2. Position is checked

    Three ultrasonic position sensors detect the vehicle and confirm it is correctly placed for weighment.

  3. Photos are captured

    C1 takes the top view for mineral identification, C2 reads the number plate, and C3 captures the digitizer and PC screen.

  4. Weight is recorded

    The weight comes from the weighbridge digitizer, and the controller ties it together with the vehicle and the photos.

  5. Data goes to eRawanna

    The complete weighment record is sent to eRawanna over the site's static IP connection.

What a site needs, as per the SOP

This is the checklist we work to at every weighbridge. We supply, install and configure the hardware. The civil work, power and internet are arranged at the site by the lease holder.

Mandatory hardware

  • RFID reader ×1 — 865 to 867 MHz, 3 to 6 m read range. One more on the other side for two-way movement.
  • Position sensors ×3 — ultrasonic, 200 to 2000 mm range, IP65.
  • IP bullet camera C1 ×1 — 2 MP, top view of the load for mineral identification.
  • IP bullet camera C2 ×1 — 4 MP varifocal, front view for number plate reading, with external IR illuminator.
  • IP bullet camera C3 ×1 — 2 MP, image of the digitizer and PC screen.
  • Controller ×1 — 8-channel isolated I/O, MODBUS over Ethernet or RTU.
  • PC ×1 — Intel i5 10th generation or higher, 16 GB RAM, 1 TB storage, Windows 11.
  • PoE network switch ×1 — 8+2 port, minimum 190 W PoE budget.

Optional hardware

  • LED display — shows messages and instructions during weighment, MQTT or API based.
  • PA system or speaker — voice announcements to guide the driver.
  • Traffic lights ×2 — red and green stop/go signals for the driver.
  • Boom barrier with safety sensor — releases the vehicle after weighment.

Mounting heights

  • Position sensors at 1500 mm from the platform.
  • Camera C1 at 7600 to 7800 mm, with all four corners of the platform visible.
  • Camera C2 at 1800 to 2000 mm, facing the incoming vehicle.
  • Camera C3 as per the height of the cabin, covering the digitizer and the PC screen.

Arranged at the site

  • Poles P1 to P4 and side railing — hot dip galvanised poles on CC foundation. P1 and P2 at 1800 to 2000 mm, P3 at 2800 to 3000 mm, P4 at 7800 to 8000 mm; railing at least 600 mm high for a pit type weighbridge.
  • Electricity supply with online UPS backup as per requirement.
  • Dedicated 20 Mbps leased line, broadband or AirFiber connection.
  • A public static IP on that connection.
  • NAT and port forwarding support on the service provider's router.

Cabling

  • 4 core shielded cable for the sensors.
  • Outdoor Cat-6 cable for the cameras and network devices.
  • RS232 cable from the weighbridge digitizer.
  • Power distribution unit and online UPS inside the cabin.

Hardware positions and the civil layout can be changed by the ME or AME as per site feasibility. We confirm the final layout with the department before starting work.
